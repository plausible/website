Public websites have a different bar to clear. Citizens don't choose to visit a tax office or a library portal the way they choose a webshop, so sending their visit data to an ad-tech company is hard to justify. And your legal, procurement and security teams all get a say before anything goes live.

Plausible is built for exactly this situation. No cookies, no personal data collection, no consent banners. All visitor data is processed and stored on EU-owned infrastructure and never leaves the EU. The Scottish and Welsh governments have been running Plausible for years, alongside ministries, municipalities, public libraries and schools across Europe.

This page covers what public-sector reviews usually ask us: where the data lives, what compliance documentation exists and how payment works if a standard card checkout isn't an option.

## Digital sovereignty, not just GDPR compliance

Many public institutions are moving away from US-owned analytics for two separate reasons. One is legal: cookie-based tools require consent banners, and data transfers to US servers keep coming under regulatory scrutiny. The other is strategic: a growing push across Europe to run public digital infrastructure on European technology.

Plausible answers both. We're an EU-based company (registered in Estonia, see our [imprint](https://plausible.io/imprint)) and your visitor data is stored in Falkenstein, Germany, on servers owned by Hetzner, a European company. Not a US hyperscaler's EU region: EU-owned infrastructure. The difference matters, and we've written about [why EU-hosted and EU-owned are not the same thing](/blog/eu-hosted-analytics-vs-eu-owned-analytics).

Plausible is also open source. Your team can audit the entire codebase, and if your requirements ever demand full infrastructure control, you can [self-host the Community Edition](https://plausible.io/self-hosted-web-analytics) on your own servers.

## No cookies means no consent banner on your public services

Plausible doesn't use cookies and doesn't collect personal data. We count visits in aggregate without building profiles of individual citizens, which means no cookie consent banner is needed for the analytics. An independent [legal assessment](https://plausible.io/blog/legal-assessment-gdpr-eprivacy) by a data protection lawyer confirms this approach.

For public services this cuts both ways in your favor: less friction for citizens using your site, and a much shorter privacy review for your DPO.

## Built for networks of public websites

Public institutions rarely run one website. A library consortium can have 200 branch sites, a school district one site per school, a ministry a whole family of campaign and service sites.

Plausible handles this from a single account. Standard plans include up to 10 sites. If you need more than 10, Enterprise plans combine a monthly usage tier for the network's total pageviews and custom events with a site allowance from one of our Enterprise tiers, sized to cover the whole network.

Within your site allowance, adding another site does not change the price or require a plan change. Some of our customers run thousands of sites on one account.

[Guest roles](https://plausible.io/docs/users-roles#working-with-external-collaborators) let you give each department or branch access to specific sites only. A [consolidated view](https://plausible.io/docs/consolidated-views) gives leadership one dashboard across the whole network, while [shared links](https://plausible.io/docs/shared-links) let you publish selected dashboards to colleagues or citizens without creating accounts.

For central access control, Enterprise plans support [Single Sign-On via SAML 2.0](https://plausible.io/docs/sso), so your staff log in through your existing identity provider.

Transparency is a natural fit here too. Any dashboard can be made fully public, so citizens can see the same traffic numbers you do. We do this ourselves: [our own stats are public](https://plausible.io/plausible.io).
